I think that when people call this a good movie, what they're actually thinking is 'Man, Thriller really is one of the greatest albums of all time'. 

This is, uh, barely a real movie. Michael 2026 is a tribute performer concert with just enough watery story to pass the time in between concert re-enactments. It's a *really good* tribute concert-- Jaafar Jackson and Juliano Krue Valdi do a great job playing older and younger Michael respectively-- but I can barely call this a biopic.

It's tragic to me (but maybe ultimately for the best) that a real biopic about Michael Jackson's career will never realistically be made. The meddling fingers and conflicting interests of real people with images and money to preserve seem to have pulled this movie in too many directions to tell very much story or history at all, and the handprints are *super* visible. It feels really bizarre if you're familiar with Michael's life. Notably: I rewatched The Wiz a few months ago, and that period of time (and, cough, Diana Ross's existence and relationship to Michael entirely, cough) being 'mysteriously' absent is super jarring to me.

The other Jackson siblings might as well have been pieces of furniture for how little the movie acknowledges them as people, much less the significant figures in Michael's life that they are. I am pretty sure his brothers, the rest of the Jackson Five (!!!) have fewer lines put together than the character of John Branca, Michael's lawyer. The J5 era as a whole feels like a purely obligatory blur to get to covering Michael's solo career.

*What the hell was up with the lawyer?* I came out of the movie wondering why on earth the movie focused on Branca so much, but it made sense when I later found out he's currently co-executor of the Jackson estate and financed the film. No hate to Miles Teller, whose performance I feel largely indifferent about, but Branca is so uncomfortably deified by the film that Teller barely registers as giving a performance to me. It's not his fault that I've seen Whiplash like a million times, but whenever he was on screen all I could think was 'why are we focusing on Boy From Whiplash again'

The portrayal of Joe Jackson was another big issue for me. He's just such a cartoon character in this, it takes some of the gravity of his real life abuse away for me. Colman Domingo *is* genuinely frightening in a sober and human way at points, but it is generally hard for me to to square him literally popping out of the shadows like a movie monster with the deeply sickening audio that has recently gone back around of Michael talking about the extents of his childhood abuse. We are shown a young Michael performing in nightclubs, and mostly made to feel bad that a kid has such a demanding schedule, but it's still strangely defanged; the movie omits these young boys being exposed to sex, drugs and alcohol in adult clubs, something that left Michael traumatized into adulthood in real life.

The point of Michael's life that the movie decides to stop at is, uh. Interesting. They do an mcu style sequel bait at the very end. I have no fucking clue how a movie about the latter half of his life is going to work, especially when his estate is clearly so interested in sanitizing his image and omitting uncomfortable real-life events. I don't think that second movie is actually going to come out, but we'll see...?
